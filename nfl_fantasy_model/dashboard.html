Where the numbers come from

Two independent inputs. Level — each player's median — comes from sportsbook season-long props, de-vigged and averaged across books. Shape — how his weeks scatter around that level — comes from the most similar historical seasons, matched on scoring level, volume, yards per reception, and touchdown dependence.

Because both are built from quantities a book prices, a rookie with no NFL history is priced exactly like a veteran. Nothing in the pipeline needs him to have played before.

Why weekly, not seasonal

Best ball starts your best lineup automatically each week, so a player's week-to-week spikiness is worth real money. Two players with identical season totals are not worth the same: the volatile one lands more of his points in weeks that actually count. Holding points per game fixed and changing only the shape moves a season total by roughly 80 points, and more at the ceiling.

Replacement level

Measured across 2014–2025 seasons rather than ranked inside this file. A props file covering 33 running backs would otherwise put "RB32" on the second-worst back in it, inflating every back on the board.

Best ball vs a managed league

The difference is one thing: when the lineup is set. Best ball picks after the week, so a bust is benched automatically. A managed league locks the lineup first, so you eat it.

Simulated on one roster, swapping a steady receiver for a volatile one at identical points per game, volatility was worth season points in best ball and in a managed league. With a locked lineup only the mean can matter, so that second number is near zero by construction, not by accident.

The catch: this shows up in roster construction, not in the draft order. Ranking every player under both formats gives orderings that correlate at 0.99. Format should change how much volatility you tolerate across twenty picks, not who you take at pick one.

Stacking

Rostering a quarterback with his own receiver never raises your expected points — correlation moves spread, not the mean. What it buys is the tail.

Odds of a top-0.1% team, against the same roster with no teammates on it. Measured over 120,000 simulated seasons using common random numbers so the difference is not buried in sampling noise.

What this does not yet do

Stacking is priced in the draft room but the board itself still ranks players one at a time. Roster construction across all 20 picks, exposure across 50 entries, and the exact payout curve of a given contest are not modelled. The managed-league figures assume a static preseason projection with no waivers and no in-season learning, which flatters best ball — treat that gap as an upper bound.
